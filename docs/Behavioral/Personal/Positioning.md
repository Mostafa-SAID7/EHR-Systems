## Conversational Techniques

### Technique 1: Tell Stories with Specific Details
**Instead of:** "I optimized system performance"
**Say:** "Patient search was taking 2 seconds - making doctors wait was poor UX. I profiled and found N+1 queries, plus ML model computing unnecessary suggestions. Fixed both together for 10x improvement."

**Why:** Specific details show you think deeply, not just claim skills

### Technique 2: Show Your Thinking Process
**Instead of:** "I chose microservices"
**Say:** "I considered three options: stay monolithic (simple but limited), strangler pattern (gradual), or clean break to microservices (risky). I chose strangler pattern because it was lowest risk while allowing learning."

**Why:** Shows you think through tradeoffs, not just follow patterns

### Technique 3: Acknowledge Constraints
**Instead of:** "Healthcare systems are easy to build"
**Say:** "Healthcare adds constraints (compliance, audit trails, regulatory) but these constraints actually improve architecture - they force good decisions about reliability and correctness."

**Why:** Shows domain understanding, not naivety

### Technique 4: Ask Good Follow-up Questions
**After explaining something:** "How does this compare to your current approach?" or "What's your biggest constraint in this area?"

**Why:** Shows genuine curiosity, not just selling yourself

### Technique 5: Connect Experience to Their Needs
**Instead of:** "I built microservices"
**Say:** "I built microservices for EHR platform with multiple services (Billing, Appointment, Audit). Your medical coding system likely needs similar architecture - independent services that coordinate without tight coupling. I've done this and understand the operational complexity."

**Why:** Shows you've thought about their specific needs

## Things NOT to Say

### ❌ Don't Oversell Yourself
**Bad:** "I'm the best engineer you'll interview"
**Good:** "I bring healthcare + microservices experience, which I think is relevant for your needs"

### ❌ Don't Bash Previous Employers
**Bad:** "My last company was disorganized"
**Good:** "I learned a lot at my last role, including both technical depth and organizational challenges"

### ❌ Don't Claim Skills You Don't Have
**Bad:** "I'm expert in ML/AI"
**Good:** "I've integrated ML models in production and understand serving challenges"

### ❌ Don't Be Defensive About Weaknesses
**Bad:** "I don't have any weaknesses"
**Good:** "I tend to over-engineer early, but I've learned to ask better questions first"

### ❌ Don't Sound Desperate
**Bad:** "I really need this job"
**Good:** "I'm excited about this opportunity and think I can contribute"
